title:  "Hedge funds are not supposed to be hedged"
---

A lot of people on Twitter are dunking on Leopold right now.

A year ago Situational Awareness was the hottest thing on the timeline. Everyone was glazing Leo, screenshotting SA's 13F and trying to copy his trades. Now that the fund is down bad, the same people are posting "a hedge fund, but we don't hedge" memes.

I'm not going to get into whether his AI view is right or wrong.

But because I keep seeing the 'hedge fund, but not hedged' meme every time a new fund blows up, I want to point out that hedge funds are not supposed to be neutral. And mostly they never are.

"How did you blow up if you're a hedge fund? Didn't you hedge?"

A bit of history. The first "hedge fund" was Alfred Winslow Jones in 1949. He bought stocks he liked and shorted stocks he didn't, so if the whole market fell he was partly covered. That fund actually was hedged.

The name has stuck though the strategy hasn't. 75 years later, "hedge fund" covers macro funds, quant funds, activist funds, credit funds, crypto funds and concentrated bets on one theme. And no, most of them aren't trying to be neutral.

"Hedge fund" means a bucket of funds, and the bucket is defined by the business model, not by the strategy.

A private equity fund buys companies, fixes them and sells them. A VC fund backs startups. A hedge fund is basically a private fund that takes a fee (the classic 2 and 20) and gets a broad mandate to trade liquid stuff however it wants. It can go long, go short, use leverage and use derivatives.

So when you call something a hedge fund, you're describing how it gets paid and what it's allowed to do. You're not saying it's market neutral.

By the way, even the guys who are supposed to stay neutral take sides too. Think market makers. Jane Street, SIG and the like are supposed to be the neutral ones. They quote both sides, earn the spread and stay flat.

In practice they lean all the time. If you're making markets on a sportsbook and you think one side is mispriced, you skew your quotes toward it. You let yourself carry some of that risk because it's +EV. (Listen to SIG's Ric on the Best Risk Takers' episode.)

If the people whose whole job is to stay neutral still take sides, why would you expect a fund that exists to have a view to sit perfectly hedged?

And being hedged doesn't mean you're safe either. LTCM was about as "hedged" as it gets. It was started by Nobel prize winners. It still blew up in 1998 and needed a bailout organised by the Fed.

Funds don't blow up because they "forgot to hedge". They blow up because of leverage, concentration and liquidity.

You can absolutely criticise Leopold. Ask about position sizing. Ask how concentrated the book was. Ask whether the drawdowns and the leverage were managed well. "Why didn't he hedge" isn't one of them. Investors put money into Situational Awareness because they wanted a concentrated AI bet. Hedging away the AI exposure would've meant giving them a different fund from the one they signed up for.

And as they say, hedging is for pussies.
