title:  "26,000 AI researcher careers"

For the last six years, top AI researchers have moved between OpenAI, Anthropic, Google DeepMind, Meta, xAI and newer labs. Most went back and forth, took nine-figure offers, while a few stayed back. 

Who played it right: the people who were loyal or the job hoppers?

To answer this we tested every path.

Note: We is doing a lot of heavy lifting here, it is mostly Claude, Gemini, and Grok.

We took one researcher who starts work in January 2020 and modelled every career they could have had across eight employers up to today. That comes to 25,756 paths, and we valued each one as of 25 November 2026.


The best path had exactly one move in it: Start at OpenAI in 2020, join Anthropic in July 2021 and never leave, and your equity would be worth about $797M today. Staying at OpenAI the whole time gets you about $104M, and staying at Google about $16M. 

What you made depended almost entirely on the share price on the day you got your equity, and on not leaving before it grew.

Why only equity? At these companies, equity is where the money is. A researcher in the model who stays from 2020 gets about $10.6M of it, valued at the time of each grant. Even a $400K salary adds up to under $3M over the same seven years, and it can't grow 300×.

To keep the math simple, we assume the researcher gets paid exactly the same everywhere. Every year they get the same dollar amount of equity at every company, and the only thing that changes is whose stock that money buys. Salary and bonus are identical too, which is why we left them out. We ignored sign-on bonuses as well. Only people who move get one, so this makes movers look slightly worse than they were. And we assume nobody sells anything. Every vested share is valued at today's price, and shares that haven't vested yet don't count.

What's left is a question of whose stock you wanted to be paid in, and when.

| Rank | Career path | Equity value today |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | OpenAI (2020) → Anthropic (Jul 2021), stays | $797M |
| 2 | Same, but goes back to OpenAI in 2024 | $479M |
| 3 | Serial hopper: OpenAI → Anthropic (2021–23) → xAI (2023–25) → Thinking Machines | $379M |
| 4 | Same hopper, ends at SSI | $371M |
| 5 | Best path that never touches Anthropic: Nvidia (2020) → OpenAI (Jan 2023) | $123M |
| 6 | OpenAI lifer | $104M |
| 7 | OpenAI → Meta (Jul 2025, start of the Meta Superintelligence hiring) | $92M |
| 8 | OpenAI → xAI (2023) | $85M |
| 9 | Nvidia lifer | $61M |
| 10 | Google / DeepMind lifer | $16M |
| 11 | Meta lifer | $15M |

The median path is worth $66M and the worst $12M. It's that high because all eight employers did well, and labs that lost their teams to bigger companies, like Inflection and Adept, aren't in the model at all.

The lifers are all over the place. The OpenAI lifer beats 70% of all paths and the Nvidia lifer 46%, while the Google and Meta lifers beat only 1% and 0.5%.

| Granted | $1 is worth today |
|---|---|
| Anthropic, mid-2021 | $322 |
| Anthropic, mid-2023 | $74 |
| OpenAI, 2020 | $28 |
| Nvidia, Jan 2020 | $25 |
| Google, Jan 2020 | $4 |
| Meta, Jan 2020 | $3 |

A dollar of Anthropic equity granted in mid-2021 is worth $322 today, which is why every path at the top of the ranking goes through Anthropic in 2021.

What I didn't expect to find is that where you started barely mattered. If you moved to Anthropic in July 2021, starting at OpenAI gets you $797M, Nvidia $794M, Google $782M and Meta $781M. When you joined Anthropic mattered a lot more:

| Joined Anthropic | Best path |
|---|---|
| Jul 2021 | $797M |
| Jan 2022 | $321M |
| Jan 2023 | $374M |
| Jan 2024 | $203M |
| Jan 2025 | $161M |

January 2023 beat January 2022 because the share price hadn't moved between those dates, and grants were bigger by 2023.

Leaving Anthropic was the expensive mistake. The serial hopper left in 2023 and kept $379M, but the unvested equity they walked away from is worth $415M today. The researcher who went back to OpenAI in 2024 kept $479M and gave up $372M. Every time you quit, you swap your unvested equity, and whatever it would have grown into, for a fresh grant somewhere else.

That doesn't make moving bad in itself. The median path with three moves ($66M) beats the median with one move ($57M), and both beat the median for staying put ($39M, from only four paths, since the other four employers didn't exist yet in 2020). More moves meant more chances to land somewhere equity was cheap. It only works because all eight employers did well, though. With a lab that failed in the mix, every extra move would also have been one more chance to land there.

Nvidia came closer to OpenAI than I expected. A 2020 OpenAI unit is up about 28× (about 40× at the reported $1.25T) and a 2020 Nvidia share is up about 25×. The lifers end up further apart, $104M against $61M, because OpenAI's later grants grew far more, but the Nvidia lifer still got about 60% as much, in shares they could sell any day.

And the 2025 mega-offers were paid in the worst stock. A $40M offer made in July 2025 is worth very different amounts today, depending on what it was paid in:

| Paid in | Priced at | Value today |
|---|---|---|
| Meta stock | $719.22 a share | ~$34M (0.85×) |
| OpenAI equity | Oct 2025 tender ($500B) | ~$58M (1.46×) |
| Anthropic equity | Sep 2025 Series F | ~$235M (5.87×) |

Meta's offers were bigger because it had to outbid the labs, but to come out ahead a Meta offer had to be about 7× an Anthropic offer, or 1.7× an OpenAI one. The real Meta packages did clear the OpenAI bar. Even paid in a falling stock, taking one roughly beat staying at OpenAI.

## Caveats

The biggest simplification is the equal grant. Nobody gets the same grant at Google as at OpenAI, and labs usually paid far more equity than Big Tech (Meta's 2025 offers were the exception). We held it equal on purpose so the comparison is only about the stock, which means the real gap between labs and Big Tech is bigger than the tables show.

Senior hires often get bought out when they switch, with extra equity to cover what they leave behind. Leaving that out makes moving look a little costlier than it was, but when we added a 20% buyout, the ranking didn't change.

Would Anthropic really have given a new hire $2M of equity in 2021? That's about 0.34% of a company valued around $0.59B, which is plausible for an early senior hire. Cap it at 0.15% and the winner falls to about $436M, still first by a wide margin.

This is all hindsight, of course. In 2021 Anthropic was a new lab started by people who had left OpenAI, and its next round was led by Sam Bankman-Fried (yes, that one). Nobody knew how it would go. I wouldn't read the ranking as a list of who was smart.

Most of this money can't be spent yet. Anthropic staff could sell only up to 10% of their locked shares on IPO day, and most of the $797M is still locked. For the winner to drop below the best path without Anthropic ($123M), Anthropic would have to fall more than 80%. OpenAI pays out only through occasional tender offers, where the company or investors buy employee shares, with per-person caps. Nvidia, Google and Meta stock is liquid.

The main results assume nobody ever sells. If the OpenAI lifer had sold 25% of their vested units at every tender, they'd have about $26M in cash and $65M in total, instead of $104M. Since every tender so far was priced at or below today's value, selling early cost money, but it did buy certainty.

All the numbers are before tax. Public-company shares are taxed as they vest and private ones mostly at a sale or IPO. After-tax totals would differ, but none of that changes the winner.

So where should you join today? I don't know, and the model can't tell you. Grants made in 2026 have mostly gone sideways so far: Anthropic's May 2026 Series H is at 1.4× after its IPO jump faded, OpenAI's March 2026 round is at 1.0×, and Nvidia shares granted in January 2026 are at 0.79×.

## How we built it & more assumptions

The researcher is a strong senior one, not a cofounder or a named star. Their going-rate grant each year is $375K (2020), $500K, $600K, $1M, $1.5M, $2.5M and $3M (2026). When they join a company they get four years of that year's rate, vesting over four years with nothing in the first year, and every work anniversary adds one more year at the current rate. When they quit, whatever hasn't vested is gone. Someone who stays from 2020 gets about $10.6M of equity in total, a $1.5M joining grant plus $9.1M of yearly top-ups.

There are eight employers: OpenAI, Anthropic, xAI, Thinking Machines, SSI, Google, Meta and Nvidia. Moves can happen every January and July from 2020, with at least 12 months at every job including the last one, which makes July 2025 the last possible move. You get up to three moves and can go back to an old employer, and a lab only shows up once it exists and has a priced funding round.

Public stocks use their real share prices at the 25 November 2026 close. Private labs are priced per share from funding rounds and filings, after dilution, because every round issues new shares and dividing valuations overstates returns by 2 to 8×. Anthropic is up about 2,600× by valuation since its Series A but 322× per share ($0.257 to $82.79, from its S-1 filing), and OpenAI is up 61× by valuation ($14B to $852B) but about 28× per unit.

For OpenAI the model uses the last fully closed round, $852B in March 2026. A $1.25T round has been reported but hasn't fully closed, and at $1.25T the OpenAI lifer would be worth about $151M while the winner barely moves ($805M). Nobody has published what a 2020 OpenAI hire paid per unit, and we used the 2021 employee tender (about $14B) instead. If 2020 units were cheaper, every OpenAI path goes up. Microsoft's 2023 investment is treated as diluting other holders, since it later became roughly a 27% stake. Treat it as non-dilutive and the OpenAI lifer goes to about $142M, with the same winner.

xAI isn't even a company any more. SpaceX bought it in February 2026 and each xAI share became SpaceX stock, so xAI equity is valued at today's SpaceX price. Its early per-share price is our estimate, worked back from the merger terms.

If a lab raised a new round within three months of your grant, your grant is priced at the new round. This stops anyone gaming the model by joining just before a big raise, and it's also why the July 2025 offers above are priced at the autumn 2025 rounds.

None of this is investment advice.
