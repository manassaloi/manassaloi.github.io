For the last six years, top AI researchers have moved between OpenAI, Anthropic, Google DeepMind, Meta, xAI and newer labs. Most went back and forth, took nine-figure offers, while some stayed back. You might wonder who played it right: the people who were loyal, or the job hoppers.

So we tested every path. 

Note: We is doing a lot of heavy lifting here, it is mostly Claude, Gemini, and Grok. 

We took one researcher who starts work in January 2020 and modelled every career they could have had across eight employers until today: 25,756 paths. We then valued each one as of 25 November 2026.

The short answer: the best path had exactly one move. Join Anthropic in July 2021, then never leave. That researcher's equity is worth about $797M today. Staying at OpenAI the whole time gives about $104M, and staying at Google gives about $16M.

What you made depended almost entirely on the share price on the day you were granted equity, and on not leaving before it grew.

## We only looked at equity. Here's why.

At these companies, equity is where the money is. In our model a researcher who stays from 2020 receives about $10.6M of equity, valued at the time each grant was made. Even a $400K salary adds up to under $3M over the same seven years, and it can't grow 300×.

To make the comparison fair, we made everything else identical:

- Same equity grant everywhere. Each year's grant is worth the same dollar amount at every company. The only thing that changes is which company's stock it buys.
- Same salary and bonus everywhere. So we leave them out, since they cancel. Sign-on bonuses are left out too. Only people who move get one, so movers come out slightly low.
- No selling. Every vested share is valued at today's price. Shares still vesting don't count.

So this isn't "who paid more". 

It's "if you got paid the same, which company's stock did you want to be paid in, and when?"

## The ranking

| Rank | Career path | Equity value today |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | OpenAI (2020) → Anthropic (Jul 2021), stays | $797M |
| 2 | Same, but goes back to OpenAI in 2024 | $479M |
| 3 | Serial hopper: OpenAI → Anthropic (2021–23) → xAI (2023–25) → Thinking Machines | $379M |
| 4 | Same hopper, ends at SSI | $371M |
| 5 | Best path that never touches Anthropic: Nvidia (2020) → OpenAI (Jan 2023) | $123M |
| 6 | OpenAI lifer | $104M |
| 7 | OpenAI → Meta (Jul 2025, start of the Meta Superintelligence hiring) | $92M |
| 8 | OpenAI → xAI (2023) | $85M |
| 9 | Nvidia lifer | $61M |
| 10 | Google / DeepMind lifer | $16M |
| 11 | Meta lifer | $15M |

Across all 25,756 paths: median $66M, best $797M, worst $12M. All eight employers did well, so the median leans high. Labs that lost their teams to bigger companies, like Inflection and Adept, aren't in it.

Where the lifers rank among all paths:

- OpenAI lifer: better than 70% of paths.
- Nvidia lifer: better than 46%.
- Google lifer: better than 1%.
- Meta lifer: better than 0.5%.

## Five things we didn't expect

### 1. Your first job barely mattered. It was all about timing your move.

If you moved to Anthropic in July 2021, where you started barely changed the result:

| First job, then Anthropic in Jul 2021 | Value today |
|---|---|
| OpenAI | $797M |
| Nvidia | $794M |
| Google | $782M |
| Meta | $781M |

The date you joined Anthropic mattered far more:

| Joined Anthropic | Best path |
|---|---|
| Jul 2021 | $797M |
| Jan 2022 | $321M |
| Jan 2023 | $374M |
| Jan 2024 | $203M |
| Jan 2025 | $161M |

Joining in January 2023 beat January 2022. The share price hadn't moved between those dates, and grants were bigger by 2023.

### 2. Leaving the winner was the expensive mistake.

The serial hopper kept $379M. The unvested equity they gave up when they quit is worth $415M today. The researcher who went back to OpenAI in 2024 kept $479M and gave up $372M.

Every time you quit, you give up your unvested equity, worth whatever it would have grown to, for a fresh grant somewhere else.

### 3. Moving isn't bad in itself.

The median path with three moves ($66M) beats the median with one move ($57M) and the median for staying put ($39M, from only four paths, since the other four employers didn't exist yet in 2020). More moves meant more chances to land somewhere where equity was cheap. That only works because all eight employers did well. Add a lab that failed, and more moves would also mean more chances to land there. What cost money was leaving Anthropic, not moving.

### 4. A 2020 Nvidia share did almost as well as a 2020 OpenAI unit.

A 2020 OpenAI unit is up about 28×, and a 2020 Nvidia share about 25×. At the reported $1.25T, the OpenAI unit would be up about 40×. The lifers end up further apart, $104M against $61M, because OpenAI's later grants grew far more. Still, the Nvidia lifer got about 60% as much, in shares they could sell any day.

### 5. The 2025 mega-offers were paid in the worst stock.

Here is what a $40M offer made in July 2025 is worth today:

| Paid in | Priced at | Value today |
|---|---|---|
| Meta stock | $719.22 a share | ~$34M (0.85×) |
| OpenAI equity | Oct 2025 tender ($500B) | ~$58M (1.46×) |
| Anthropic equity | Sep 2025 Series F | ~$235M (5.87×) |

Meta's offers were bigger because it had to outbid the labs. To come out ahead, though, a Meta offer had to be about 7× an Anthropic offer, or 1.7× an OpenAI one. Meta's real packages cleared the OpenAI bar: taking one still roughly beat staying at OpenAI, even paid in a falling stock. The headline number is only half an offer; the other half is the stock it's paid in.

## Questions you're probably asking

"Nobody gets the same grant at Google as at OpenAI."

True. Labs usually paid far more equity than Big Tech; Meta's 2025 offers were the exception. We held it equal on purpose so you can compare the stocks. In real life the gap between labs and Big Tech is bigger than our table shows.

"Who is this researcher?"

A strong senior researcher, not a cofounder or a named star.

- Going-rate grant each year: $375K (2020), $500K, $600K, $1M, $1.5M, $2.5M, $3M (2026).
- When you join: you get four years of that year's rate. It vests over four years, with nothing in the first year.
- Each work anniversary: adds one more year at the current rate.
- When you quit: whatever hasn't vested is gone.

Someone who stays from 2020 receives about $10.6M of equity in total: a $1.5M joining grant plus $9.1M of yearly top-ups.

"Don't people get bought out when they switch?"

Often, yes: senior hires get extra equity to cover what they leave behind. We leave that out, so moving looks a little costlier than it was. Adding a 20% buyout didn't change the ranking.

"Would Anthropic really have given a new hire $2M of equity in 2021?"

That's about 0.34% of a company valued around $0.59B, which is plausible for an early senior hire. If we cap it at 0.15%, the winner falls to about $436M. It's still number one by a wide margin.

"Isn't this just hindsight?"

Yes. In 2021 Anthropic was a new lab started by people who had left OpenAI, and its next round was led by Sam Bankman-Fried. Nobody knew. This post shows where risk got paid, not who was smart. We also don't model labs that were absorbed by bigger companies, like Inflection and Adept.

"Can they actually spend this money?"

Mostly not yet.

- Anthropic staff could sell up to 10% of locked shares on IPO day. Most of the $797M is still locked. Anthropic would have to fall more than 80% before the winner dropped below the best path without Anthropic ($123M).
- OpenAI pays out only through occasional tender offers, where the company or investors buy employee shares, with per-person caps.
- Nvidia, Google and Meta stock is liquid.

"Should they have sold at OpenAI's tender offers?"

This is a separate scenario; the main results assume you never sell. If the OpenAI lifer had sold 25% of vested units at every tender, they'd have about $26M in cash and $65M in total, instead of $104M. Every tender so far was priced at or below today's value, so selling early cost money. It also bought certainty.

"Why don't you just divide valuations?"

Because every funding round issues new shares.

- Anthropic: about 2,600× by valuation since its Series A, but 322× per share ($0.257 → $82.79, from its S-1 filing).
- OpenAI: 61× by valuation ($14B → $852B), about 28× per unit.

Dividing valuations overstates returns by 2–8×.

"What price did you use for OpenAI? It's private."

Its last fully closed round: $852B (March 2026). A $1.25T round has been reported but hasn't fully closed. At $1.25T the OpenAI lifer is worth about $151M. The winner barely moves ($805M).

"What did a 2020 OpenAI hire actually pay per unit?"

That price isn't public. We used the 2021 employee tender (about $14B). If 2020 units were cheaper, every OpenAI path goes up.

"What about the Microsoft deal?"

We treat Microsoft's 2023 investment as diluting other holders, since it later became roughly a 27% stake. If you treat it as non-dilutive, the OpenAI lifer goes to about $142M. The winner doesn't change.

"xAI isn't a company any more."

SpaceX bought it in February 2026. Each xAI share became SpaceX stock, so we value xAI equity at today's SpaceX price. xAI's early per-share price is our estimate, worked back from the merger terms.

"Couldn't you game it by joining just before a big funding round?"

We blocked that. If a lab raised a new round within three months of your grant, your grant is priced at the new round. That's also why a July 2025 offer is priced at the autumn 2025 rounds above.

"What about taxes?"

All numbers are before tax. Public-company shares are taxed as they vest, and private ones mostly at a sale or IPO, so after-tax totals would differ. None of that changes the winner.

"So where should I join today?"

This isn't a prediction. Grants made in 2026 have mostly gone sideways so far:

- Anthropic's May 2026 Series H: 1.4×, after its IPO jump faded.
- OpenAI's March 2026 round: 1.0×.
- Nvidia shares granted in January 2026: 0.79×.

## How we built it

- Employers: OpenAI, Anthropic, xAI, Thinking Machines, SSI, Google, Meta and Nvidia.
- Moves: possible every January and July from 2020, with at least 12 months at every job, including the last one. So the last possible move is July 2025.
- Limits: up to three moves, and you can go back to an old employer. A lab only appears once it exists and has a priced funding round.
- Private labs: priced per share from funding rounds and filings, after dilution.
- Public stocks: priced at their real share prices, valued at the 25 Nov 2026 close.

This is a model, not advice. Prices as of 25 November 2026.
